If it falls through

Whitefish is decided. It's 19 minutes from the airport, 39 from the park entrance, and the only town in the valley with a real main street — which matters more than usual on this trip, because nothing inside the park is open for food and the ski mountain hasn't woken up yet. Mid-October is a genuine dead zone here: Whitefish Mountain closed Sept 20 and doesn't reopen until Dec 4, and park visitation drops to 18% of its July peak. That's why it's cheap, and also why you should check that your restaurant is open before you drive to it. The four other towns are still below for the record — and because a cabin in Columbia Falls is genuinely cheaper if the Whitefish inventory dries up.

Booking notes

  • Mid-October is a true shoulder trough: Whitefish Mountain closed Sept 20 and reopens Dec 4, and Glacier's October visitation is 18% of its July peak — an 82% drop.
  • The one published seasonal table in the valley (Great Northern, Coram) shows off-season at 36-39% below peak. Expect a real rate discount nearer 35% than the 80% the occupancy collapse might suggest.
  • Montana charges 8% lodging tax statewide. Whitefish adds a 3% resort tax (11% total); Columbia Falls, Kalispell, Bigfork and West Glacier add nothing. On a 4-night stay that gap is about $60 — do not pick a town over it.
  • Cleaning runs $325+ per turnover for a 4BR in this market, and that's the cleaner's price to the host, so the guest-facing fee is usually higher. On four nights it's still the single biggest add-on.
  • Book by phone. Vacay Whitefish (406-609-9595) has the deepest published 4-5BR inventory in both Whitefish and Columbia Falls; Stay Montana (888-871-7856) holds the big Bigfork lakefront homes.
  • Anything sleeping 10 usually means 4-5 bedrooms, and Airbnb's guest count includes sofa beds — read the actual bed list.
