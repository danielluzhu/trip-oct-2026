The plan

Three nights in Paradise Valley

Based in Paradise Valley / Livingston · Fri Oct 16 - Sun Oct 19, 2026 (flexible, 3-5 days)

Bozeman is the hub, but Paradise Valley is the base: best-value housing, 30-45 minutes from Livingston's restaurants, an hour from Yellowstone's north gate. The shape is deliberately loose — one big fishing day, one big park day, and two travel days that don't try to do much. On the lasso plan: it's essentially a dead end in October. Every guest ranch that teaches roping runs summer-only, and the Gardiner and Paradise Valley outfitters send their whole horse string to hunting camps from September. Horseback riding is very much on.

Day 1 · Fri 10/16 (Arrival)

Land, provision, soak. Nothing ambitious — people are arriving from three time zones.
  1. Midday
    Land at BZN, collect two SUVs ~$1,370 for 4 days, all in
    One 7-seat SUV will not fit 8 people plus luggage, and two vehicles lets the group split later in the weekend. No agency guarantees a drivetrain, so call the counter about AWD.
  2. ~1 PM
    Costco run, 2505 Catron St
    Ten minutes from the terminal and on your way out of town. Load up on breakfasts, lunches, beer and snacks — Montana charges no sales tax on groceries, while Bozeman restaurants run 25-50% above the US average.
  3. ~2:30 PM
    Drive south to the house
    About 50 minutes via I-90 and US-89 through Paradise Valley. Fuel up before you leave the Gallatin Valley.
  4. Optional, 12:30-1 PM
    Horseback at the Chico barn $145 pp / hr, $215 for two
    Rockin' HK runs 1-hour rides at 10:00 and 13:00 and 2-hour rides at 09:30 and 12:30, and their live booking calendar shows all four of your days open. Ages 8+, with firm weight limits (250 lb at 6ft, 175 lb at 5'5"). Only works if you land early — otherwise make it Saturday's alternative for anyone skipping the boats.
  5. Late afternoon
    Soak at Chico Hot Springs, Pray MT price unconfirmed
    About 35 minutes from Livingston. ⚠️ Day-use pool price, hours and whether non-guests need a reservation are all unconfirmed — call (406) 333-4933. That same call books dinner and the horses.
  6. 5-9 PM
    Dinner, Chico Historic Dining Room
    Open daily, including Sunday and Monday — which matters, because most of the region isn't. Online booking caps at 6, so a party of 8 has to phone (406) 333-4933.
  7. After dinner
    Movie night: Brokeback Mountain
    Of your three, it's the only one set in this landscape — Wyoming ranch country, a couple of hours south. Old Boy and Internal Affairs are both better films and both belong to a different trip.

Day 2 · Sat 10/17

The fishing day, and the only one where the whole group has dawn to dark free.
  1. Before you leave home
    Buy licences online $31.50 pp
    Non-resident: $10 conservation licence + $7.50 AIS pass + $14 for a one-day base = $31.50 each. There is no 3-day non-resident licence; the next tier is 5 days at $73.50. Buy at ols.fwp.mt.gov rather than burning a morning in a shop.
  2. 8 AM
    Guided float — four boats ~$2,800 + 15-20% tips
    Sweetwater Fly Shop in Livingston posts $700 per boat for a full day: two anglers, lunch, flies and loaner rods included. Montana Angler is $740, The River's Edge $675. Booking four boats on an October Saturday should be easy, but call rather than book online.
  3. Cheaper alternative
    Paradise Valley spring creeks $50/rod/day
    DePuy and Armstrong both drop to $50 per rod per day on Oct 15 — your dates land just inside the cheap window. Flat, clear, walk-in wading with no boat needed. Eight rods is $400 for the day. The wading is easy but the fishing is technically demanding, so pair it with a guide.
  4. Worth knowing
    October is a big-fish month, not a many-fish month
    The fall brown trout run is on and the crowds are gone. But Montana Angler's own guidance is that catch rates are no better than other seasons — which is a real tradeoff for a group of first-timers, who generally have more fun with numbers than with trophy hunting. Ask the shop directly whether beginners will do better on a float or on the creeks.
  5. Evening
    Dinner in Livingston
    Rib & Chop House has event space that's free to reserve, (406) 222-9200. Yellowstone Valley Grill books parties up to 10 straight through online and has a private wine cave — ⚠️ but one listing suggests it runs only April through October, so confirm on (406) 333-4162. 2nd Street Bistro is open Saturday, closed Sunday and Monday.

Day 3 · Sun 10/18

Yellowstone. Early, long, and the one day worth setting an alarm for.
  1. 5:00-5:30 AM
    Leave for Lamar Valley
    Livingston to Gardiner is 65 minutes, Gardiner to Lamar another 65. You want to be parked in Lamar at first light, because that's when wolves move.
  2. At the gate
    Entrance fee $70
    $35 per vehicle for seven days, so $70 for two — cheaper than a single $80 America the Beautiful pass. ⚠️ Anyone in the group who isn't a US resident pays a $100 surcharge unless they hold an annual pass, which flips that maths entirely. No vehicle reservation is needed.
  3. Dawn
    Lamar Valley — wolves, and bison
    NPS calls the northern range one of the best places in the world to watch wolves; the park holds between 83 and 123. Bring or rent a spotting scope — they're watched at long distance and binoculars alone will disappoint. Bison are abundant here year-round, though the rut is July and August, not now.
  4. Heads up
    Lamar is an out-and-back
    Dunraven Pass, between Canyon and Tower, closed Oct 12 — so you can't loop back that way. Beartooth Highway and the Chief Joseph Byway closed the same day, which makes Cooke City a dead end. The Gardiner-to-Cooke City road itself is open year-round.
  5. On the way
    Fuel in Gardiner, not Mammoth
    The Mammoth gas station shut for the season on Oct 4, and Roosevelt Lodge has been closed since Sept 7, so there are no services at Tower. Also budget for up to 15-minute delays at the Gardner River High Bridge just southeast of Mammoth — that construction runs 24 hours a day into late October, and it sits directly on your route.
  6. Late afternoon
    Mammoth Hot Springs on the way out
    The terraces, the Albright Visitor Center (open year-round) and elk on the lawns. The rut runs early September to mid-October, so you're catching the tail end — bulls will still be around, but peak bugling is probably over. Don't build the day around it.
  7. Evening
    Dinner — Sunday narrows the field
    Little Star Diner in Bozeman books parties up to 10 online and serves daily 5-9 PM, Sundays included. Chico's dining room is the other reliable Sunday option. 2nd Street Bistro is closed.

Day 4 · Mon 10/19 (Departure)

Pack, one last thing in Bozeman, fly home. Keep it loose.
  1. Morning
    Out of the house, north to Bozeman
    Roughly 50-65 minutes. Leave buffer: Bozeman Pass is a designated chain-up area and nights sit below freezing, so morning black ice is a genuine possibility.
  2. If there's time
    Museum of the Rockies unconfirmed
    ⚠️ Unverified — I could not confirm admission price, October hours, or whether the T. rex hall is open rather than under renovation. Call before driving over. If it's a bust, Bozeman Spirits on Main runs distillery tours, though they publish no price.
  3. The other option
    Hyalite Canyon — but check first
    ⚠️ Hyalite Canyon Road has a seasonal fall vehicle closure and mid-October is plausibly inside it. If the gate is shut, Palisade Falls is off the table. Custer Gallatin National Forest, Bozeman Ranger District: (406) 522-2520.
  4. Last meal
    Lunch before the airport
    Montana Ale Works doesn't open until 4 PM, so it's out. Backcountry Burger Bar does burgers at $11-16. Bar 3 BBQ is walk-in rather than reservations, so it's a gamble with eight.
